<p>
Stimuli-responsive actuators respond to external cues such as water uptake, thermal excitation, and electric fields to perform mechanical work. A primary goal of our research is to utilize the fundamental principles of polymer mechanics to design stimuli-responsive actuators. 
</p>

## 1. Swelling-Based Actuators and Sensors

The actuator is made of two components: a soft active (responsive) gel that undergoes significant volumetric expansion upon swelling and a non-responsive structural element 3D-printed elastic rod. In the proposed design, only the active gel component needs to be immersed in water to trigger swelling and during the whole experiment, while the stiff non-responsive rod serves as the load-bearing element. The actuator design is easily fabricated by leveraging the flexibility achieved by 3D-printing.
Environmental moisture can be harnessed as a powerful energy source for soft actuation.

## 2. Thermo-Mechanical Activation and Control
Beyond hydration, thermal stimuli provide a high-speed, reversible method for controlling soft actuators.

<div style="display: flex; flex-wrap: wrap; gap: 20px; align-items: center; margin-top: 20px;">
  <div style="flex: 1; min-width: 300px; order: 2;">
    <p> 
      Our group focuses on the <strong>thermally induced activation</strong> of PNIPAM-based composites. Key research areas include:
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Constitutive Modeling:</strong> Developing energy-based frameworks that couple temperature-dependent phase transitions with finite-deformation mechanics.</li>
        <li><strong>Programmed Morphing:</strong> Designing lattice architectures that translate isotropic thermal expansion (or contraction) into complex 3D shapes.</li>
        <li><strong>Dynamic Response:</strong> Investigating the stability and speed of thermally triggered actuators under varying mechanical loads and heat transfer rates.</li>
      </ul>  
    </p>
